What is Healing History

History repeats itself because of all the unprocessed historical trauma that cycles. Healing History breaks that cycle by helping people, groups, and countries process their historical trauma and learn positive coping skills, effective systemic change, and how to live trauma-free — and help create a world with less trauma.

Healing History is a series of seminars that can be attended one by one, or as a whole training and certificate program made up of many different seminars and other requirements. Seminars are held in many countries — online in some, in person in others — and taught by a team of teachers. Depending on the country, classes are sometimes online, sometimes in person, or both.

Background

The concept of this training is based on our experience of increasing fear and insecurity, in the growing right-wing extremism and racism in Europe and worldwide, as well as an escalating sense of ‘us versus them’ and a deep yearning to find new patterns in a more and more complex world. We want to bring together people of different backgrounds, faiths, and political interests in order to create an atmosphere of mutual trust and understanding across national boundaries. We want to reduce prejudice against other ethnic groups and beliefs, ease “xenophobia,” and facilitate conflicts. Healing History is active in many parts of the world, including Eastern and Western Europe, parts of Africa, Asia, the United States, and Canada.

Previous achievements

Healing History has played an active role in more than 14 countries. We started with our work in Israel and Palestine, expanded to Eastern and Western Europe and the United States and Canada, and are now also active in several countries in Asia, Africa, and many other places.

Healing History — creating the better future

One day my wife and I were walking down the streets of Tel Aviv, and an older man came up to us and said …

“Can you please listen to me. I need to tell someone what happened to me and my family during World War II, and how I escaped just at the last possible moment to Israel.”

We listened to his whole story. I knew that meeting was the beginning of a new direction for our work — and it led me to become director of the Healing History Project.

Why we started

Three moments that started this work

A stranger in Tel Aviv asked only to be heard — to tell someone what his family survived in the war.

Whether or not Jews were directly threatened in the Holocaust, are second-generation survivors, or come from families who weren’t directly impacted, all Jewish people carry part of this history. Many aren’t aware of how it shapes their daily life and their world perspective. Jewish people need the opportunity to explore this history with supportive facilitation.

A German woman working on her family’s SS history carried the same unprocessed cloud as a survivor’s daughter.

During a seminar in Israel, a German woman worked on her family history in the SS — the trauma, the depression, the cloud of unprocessed history that hung over her family. A Jewish woman whose parents were in the concentration camps said the atmosphere in her own family felt the same. So many people in Europe, the German woman said, need the chance to work on what they carry from World War II.

Election posters for a right-wing Nazi party in Austria showed the old hatreds returning.

Working in Austria, I saw election posters everywhere for a candidate from a right-wing Nazi party. I saw anti-Semitism and other Nazi positions coming forward again, and knew something must be done to address it.

Healing History · Europe

Traveling workshops across Europe

We began a series of traveling presentations and workshops throughout Europe — helping people process the history of their own country alongside others from related nations, and see how unprocessed history shapes our minds, bodies, and the relationships between groups.

  1. 2013Warsaw — the first workshop
  2. 2014Bonn, Germany
  3. thenBrussels & Budapest
  4. nextRequests from Croatia, Greece & Ukraine

The first workshop was in Warsaw in 2013. People from Jewish, Polish, Russian, German, and other backgrounds attended this dynamic journey into healing.

In Bonn in 2014 we again worked with many pieces of unprocessed history — and how it affects our minds, bodies, and the relationships between different groups.

The next workshops were in Brussels and Budapest — and already requests for more have come in from Croatia, Greece, Ukraine, and other countries.

Much of what we suffer as individual psychological problems is not only personal; it also carries a collective piece of unprocessed history.

History that is not delved into, thought about, felt, and learned from tends to repeat itself.

How we work

Process Work & Worldwork

Our method is Process Oriented Psychology and its approach to conflict, called Worldwork — one framework that joins personal psychology, social change, small- and large-group work, inner work, and trauma and family work.

It rests on deep democracy: the belief that every perspective, feeling, and political position matters and must be worked with to build lasting peace. We enter the tension completely — not with weapons, but with awareness, structure, and facilitation.

Used to work on history and conflict in Ukraine, Russia, Kenya, South Africa, the United States, Japan, Northern Ireland, Israel, Palestine, Brazil, and many other countries.

Healing History · Israel & Palestine

Twenty years in the fire of conflict

For twenty straight years we have gathered Palestinians and Israelis — at the Tantur Center in Jerusalem and the Everest Center in Beit Jalla — in four-day workshops that both process the ongoing conflict and train local people to facilitate it in their own communities.

  1. startFirst gatherings — a room half Israeli, half Palestinian
  2. 20 yrsTantur, Jerusalem · Everest, Beit Jalla
  3. alsoFaradis, Nazareth & Sakhnin
  4. nowThe children of participants join

We were crowded into a room together — many Palestinians and Israelis, our team facilitating. Two men spent much of the time telling their traumatic stories, with anger, hurt, and grief.

We work to identify the polarities and roles in the conflict, stay with the moments that are hottest or most cooling, and help each side find some essence-level common ground.

Peace can’t be made only by solving problems when there is so much hurt, distrust, and history between people. By letting the hardest experiences come forward and be worked with safely, peace can be made in the mind and heart as well as in practice.

A young Palestinian girl who was too depressed to function began to dance with joy at our seminars. A young man who had wanted to become a suicide bomber chose instead to go back to school and help his people.

The Israeli man walked toward the Palestinian man and told him not to forget that he loves him. The Palestinian man told him not to forget that he loves him too — and they embraced.

Healing History has 3 main activities we focus on.

01

Process history

Giving people the opportunities to process their history in a community based, safe, facilitated setting.

02

Train facilitators

Training people in these methods they can take back to their families, communities, and countries to help break the cycle of repeating history and war.

03

Certify trainers

Creating a training program that will certify trainers in each region to carry this work forward.
